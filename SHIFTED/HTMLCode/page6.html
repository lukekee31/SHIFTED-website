Boxer Engine

Balanced, stable and instantly recognisable. The Boxer engine's unique horizontal layout provides excellent balance and a lower centre of gravity, making it popular in Porsche and Subaru vehicles.

Overview

A Boxer engine positions its cylinders horizontally opposite each other. As the pistons move, they travel in opposite directions, resembling two boxers throwing punches, which is how the engine earned its name.

This layout naturally balances engine vibrations while lowering the engine within the chassis. As a result, Boxer engines are well known for excellent handling, stability and smoothness. Although less common than Inline and V engines, Boxer engines remain an important part of Porsche and Subaru's identity.

How a Boxer Engine is Arranged

Unlike Inline or V engines, Boxer engines place cylinders flat against each other. Opposing pistons move in opposite directions simultaneously, naturally cancelling many engine vibrations.

Advantages

Excellent Balance

Opposing pistons reduce vibration naturally.

Low Centre of Gravity

Improves cornering and vehicle stability.

Better Handling

Weight sits lower in the vehicle.

Smooth Operation

Minimal engine vibration without requiring additional balancing shafts.

Disadvantages

Wider Engine

Requires more space across the engine bay.

More Expensive

More difficult to manufacture than Inline engines.

Maintenance Difficulty

Spark plugs and cylinder heads may be harder to access.

Limited Adoption

Only a small number of manufacturers continue to use Boxer engines.

Why Porsche & Subaru Continue Using Boxer Engines

Porsche

The Boxer engine allows Porsche to keep the engine low in the chassis, improving handling while maintaining the characteristic driving feel of the 911.

Subaru

Subaru combines its Boxer engines with Symmetrical All-Wheel Drive to improve vehicle balance, stability and traction in all weather conditions.

Quick Facts

  • Invented over 100 years ago.
  • Naturally balanced design.
  • Lower centre of gravity than Inline or V engines.
  • Primarily used by Porsche and Subaru today.
  • Also known as a Flat Engine.

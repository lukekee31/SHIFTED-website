Engine Power

Internal combustion engines have powered cars for over a century. Learn how they work, why different layouts exist and what makes each engine unique.

The Basics

Turning fuel into motion

An engine's job is simple to state but complex to achieve: convert the chemical energy in fuel into the mechanical motion that turns the wheels. How this happens — and how the cylinders are arranged inside the engine — varies between manufacturers and vehicle types.

Different engine layouts exist because each one balances smoothness, size, cost and performance differently, making them suited to different kinds of cars.

How It Works

The Four-Stroke Cycle

Intake

The piston moves down, drawing an air-fuel mixture into the cylinder.

Compression

The piston moves up, compressing the mixture tightly.

Power

A spark ignites the mixture, forcing the piston back down.

Exhaust

The piston rises again, pushing spent gases out.

The Main Feature

Engine Layout Explorer

Compare the three most common cylinder layouts used in cars today.

Inline Engine

Cylinders are arranged in a single straight row. It's the simplest, most compact and most cost-effective layout to manufacture.

Advantages

Compact, lightweight, inexpensive to produce.

Disadvantages

Less smooth than a V or Boxer at high cylinder counts.

Manufacturers: Toyota, Honda, BMW

Example cars: Corolla, Civic, Mazda 3

BMW is famous for its smooth inline-six engines, prized for decades by driving enthusiasts.

V Engine

Two banks of cylinders are set at an angle to each other, forming a "V" shape. This allows more cylinders in a shorter engine.

Advantages

High power potential, smooth at high cylinder counts.

Disadvantages

Wider, heavier and more expensive to build.

Manufacturers: Ferrari, Mercedes-AMG, Ford

Example cars: Mustang GT, Ferrari F8, AMG GT

V engines power most modern supercars thanks to their high power density.

Boxer Engine

Cylinders lie flat and oppose each other horizontally, "punching" like boxing gloves. This creates an extremely low centre of gravity.

Advantages

Very low centre of gravity, excellent balance.

Disadvantages

Wide engine bay, costly to manufacture and service.

Manufacturers: Subaru, Porsche

Example cars: WRX, BRZ / GR86, Porsche 911

Subaru has built boxer engines for decades, prized for their low, balanced weight distribution.
